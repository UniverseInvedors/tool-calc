BMI categories

What does your BMI mean?

BMI categories are defined by the World Health Organization and widely used by healthcare professionals worldwide.

Below 18.5
Underweight
May indicate insufficient nutrition or an underlying health condition. A doctor can assess the cause.
18.5 – 24.9
Normal weight
Associated with lower risk of chronic disease. Maintain through balanced diet and regular physical activity.
25.0 – 29.9
Overweight
Slightly elevated risk for type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Lifestyle changes are often beneficial.
30.0 and above
Obese
Higher risk of serious health conditions. Medical guidance is recommended for weight management strategies.

How it works

The BMI formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m)
Example: A person who weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall:
BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9 (Normal weight)

Important note

Limitations of BMI

Not for athletes
Muscle weighs more than fat. Highly muscular individuals may have a high BMI but very low body fat — BMI cannot distinguish between the two.
Age and gender matter
BMI thresholds for healthy weight differ in children, teenagers, and the elderly. Standard adult BMI categories apply to adults 18–65.
Ethnic differences
Studies show that people of Asian origin may have higher health risks at lower BMI values. Some health guidelines use adjusted thresholds for different ethnicities.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a healthy BMI?
For adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered normal weight according to the World Health Organization. Below 18.5 is underweight. 25–29.9 is overweight. 30 and above is obese.
How accurate is BMI?
BMI is a useful population-level screening tool, but it is not perfectly accurate at the individual level. It doesn't account for muscle mass, bone density, fat distribution, age, gender, or ethnicity. For a full health assessment, consult a healthcare professional.
